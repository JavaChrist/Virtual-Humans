# 1. Purpose

This document defines the official camera rules for Mei.

It governs:

* framing;
* camera distance;
* camera height;
* camera angle;
* lens selection;
* perspective;
* composition;
* focus;
* depth of field;
* camera movement;
* presenter positioning;
* image-to-video consistency;
* visual continuity;
* provider adaptation;
* camera quality assurance;
* AI Command Center OS integration.

This document defines **how Mei must be filmed, framed or generated by a virtual camera**.

It does not redefine:

* Mei’s appearance;
* her personality;
* her wardrobe;
* her voice;
* her expressions;
* her poses;
* the visual identity of a brand;
* editing and publishing rules.

Those elements are defined in their respective SDK files.

# 3. Camera identity principle

The camera must support Mei’s identity.

It must not redefine her through:

* perspective distortion;
* excessive lens compression;
* extreme angles;
* artificial body proportions;
* inconsistent scale;
* inappropriate intimacy;
* accidental sexualization;
* unstable framing.

The camera is a production tool.

It is not allowed to become a source of character drift.

---

# 4. Camera classification system

Every camera rule belongs to one of three classes.

## 4.1 LOCKED

A locked camera rule must be respected in all standard productions.

Examples:

* no extreme facial distortion;
* no unapproved fisheye;
* no hidden face in presenter content;
* no inconsistent character scale.

## 4.2 CONTROLLED

A controlled camera element may vary according to:

* platform;
* scene;
* content type;
* visual objective;
* movement;
* environment;
* product;
* background.

## 4.3 CONTEXTUAL

A contextual camera choice is specific to one shot or sequence.

Examples:

* temporary low angle;
* product close-up;
* tracking shot;
* over-the-shoulder framing;
* establishing shot.

# 7. Camera height

Classification:

```text
LOCKED DEFAULT / CONTROLLED VARIATION
```

Default camera height:

```text
At Mei’s eye level
```

Allowed tolerance:

```text
Approximately ±10 cm relative to eye level
```

Eye-level framing is preferred because it:

* preserves facial geometry;
* supports direct communication;
* avoids dominance;
* avoids submission;
* reduces perspective distortion;
* remains compatible with presenter content.

# 8. High-angle camera

Classification:

```text
CONTROLLED
```

A high-angle shot may be used for:

* interface demonstrations;
* seated scenes;
* product handling;
* environmental composition;
* creative transitions.

The angle should remain moderate.

Avoid:

* making Mei appear childlike;
* reducing her authority excessively;
* enlarging the head;
* shortening the body;
* hiding the jawline;
* creating unnatural facial proportions.

An extreme overhead shot requires explicit direction.

---

# 9. Low-angle camera

Classification:

```text
CONTROLLED HIGH-RISK
```

A moderate low angle may be used for:

* automotive content;
* walking scenes;
* dynamic introductions;
* architectural environments;
* confident campaign imagery.

It must not:

* enlarge the lower body;
* distort the face;
* create an authoritarian presence;
* produce an accidental sexualized angle;
* expose unintended areas;
* make hands or feet oversized.

Extreme low angles are prohibited by default.

---

# 10. Dutch angle

Classification:

```text
RESTRICTED
```

A tilted horizon may be used only for:

* a short creative transition;
* a fictional narrative;
* deliberate tension;
* a dynamic social-media effect.

It must not be used for:

* tutorials;
* support;
* product demonstrations;
* legal content;
* onboarding;
* standard commercial presentation.

The camera should return quickly to a stable horizon.

---

# 11. Eye-line

Classification:

```text
CRITICAL CONTROLLED
```

For direct presenter content, Mei should look:

```text
Toward the camera lens
```

Allowed eye-line variations:

* toward a product;
* toward a screen;
* toward another character;
* toward a direction of movement;
* briefly away during reflection;
* toward a contextual object.

Mei must return to camera when addressing the audience directly.

# 12. Direct-to-camera framing

Direct-to-camera content should preserve:

* clearly visible eyes;
* natural head position;
* centered or intentionally offset composition;
* stable eye-line;
* enough headroom;
* no facial obstruction;
* no lens distortion.

This is the default for:

* introductions;
* tutorials;
* calls to action;
* commercial presentations;
* onboarding;
* explanations.

---

# 13. Camera distance

Camera distance must remain coherent with:

* lens choice;
* framing;
* environment;
* intended emotional proximity;
* product scale.

The virtual camera must not simulate an extreme close lens merely to fill the frame.

When the frame becomes tighter, the camera should generally move back and use a longer focal length rather than approaching too closely.

# 17. Full-body shot

Classification:

```text
OFFICIAL REFERENCE FRAMING
```

A full-body shot includes:

* full head;
* full torso;
* both legs;
* both feet;
* sufficient margin above and below.

Required:

* no cropped shoes;
* no cut-off hair;
* stable body proportions;
* natural perspective;
* visible posture;
* correct footwear;
* correct shadow contact.

Recommended camera height:

```text
Between waist level and chest level, with minimal tilt,
or at eye level from a sufficient distance.
```

The chosen setup must not distort the legs or head.

# 25. Headroom

Default headroom should be:

```text
Balanced and minimal
```

Avoid:

* excessive empty space above the head;
* hair touching the top edge without intention;
* inconsistent headroom between matching shots;
* accidental cropping of hair.

Headroom may be reduced for a close-up but must remain visually comfortable.

---

# 26. Lead room

When Mei looks or moves to one side, the frame should normally preserve space in the direction of:

* gaze;
* movement;
* gesture;
* product presentation.

Insufficient lead room can make the composition feel constrained or incorrect.

Intentional negative lead room requires a specific creative reason.

---

# 27. Safe margins

Composition must preserve safe areas for:

* subtitles;
* social-media overlays;
* platform controls;
* logos;
* calls to action;
* cropping between aspect ratios.

Mei’s face should not be placed under interface elements.

The final platform template has priority for exact safe zones.

# 48. Perspective distortion

Blocking perspective defects include:

* enlarged nose;
* stretched forehead;
* compressed jaw;
* oversized hands;
* elongated feet;
* widened hips caused by lens position;
* inconsistent leg length;
* curved body lines;
* unnatural head-to-body ratio.

Perspective must preserve Mei’s official anatomy.

---

# 49. Camera-to-subject distance

The camera should maintain enough distance to preserve natural geometry.

For face-focused content:

```text
Prefer greater distance with a longer focal length
instead of extreme physical proximity.
```

For full-body content:

```text
Use sufficient distance to minimize vertical perspective distortion.
```

---

# 50. Focus priority

Classification:

```text
CRITICAL LOCKED
```

Default focus priority:

```text
Nearest visible eye
```

When both eyes are similarly distant, both should appear sufficiently sharp.

Alternative focus targets are allowed for:

* product detail;
* intentional reveal;
* rack focus;
* narrative transition.

When Mei is speaking directly to camera, the eyes must remain the primary focus.

---

# 51. Face focus

The face must remain evaluable.

Prohibited:

* focus behind Mei;
* sharp hair but soft eyes;
* one eye unintentionally blurred in a standard presenter shot;
* focus pulsing during speech;
* loss of focus during simple movement.

---

# 55. Bokeh

Bokeh should be:

* natural;
* subtle;
* lens-consistent;
* free of artificial patterns;
* stable in video.

* excessive circular artifacts;

* changing bokeh geometry;
* background objects dissolving unnaturally;
* bokeh overlapping the subject.

# 56. Motion blur

Classification:

```text
CONTROLLED
```

Allowed:

* hair movement;

Prohibited:

* synthetic smearing;

# 57. Shutter appearance

For natural video motion, target a visual equivalent close to:

```text
180-degree shutter behavior
```

Examples:

```text
25 fps → approximately 1/50 s
30 fps → approximately 1/60 s
50 fps → approximately 1/100 s
60 fps → approximately 1/120 s
```

Exact provider controls may differ.

The visual objective is natural motion, not strict metadata compliance.

* mechanical constant-speed motion when visually inappropriate;
* sudden direction changes without cause;
* floating camera behavior.

---

# 75. Camera path continuity

In generated video, the camera path must remain spatially coherent.

Avoid:

* teleportation;
* unexplained lens changes;
* shifting camera height;
* background geometry changes;
* subject scale jumps;
* inconsistent direction of movement;
* impossible collision with objects.

---

# 76. Lens continuity

Within a continuous shot, lens behavior must remain stable.

Prohibited:

* unrequested focal-length shifts;
* facial compression changes;
* sudden wide-angle distortion;
* changing depth of field without a focus or lens event;
* background scale mutation.

A deliberate zoom must be specified.

---

# 77. Shot-to-shot lens strategy

Different shots may use different lenses, but the sequence should remain visually coherent.

Example:

```text
Wide establishing shot: 35 mm
Medium presenter shot: 50 mm
Close-up: 85 mm
Product detail: macro
```

The change must support the edit.

It must not make Mei appear physically different.

---

# 78. Matching angles

For continuity editing, matching shots must preserve:

* camera side;
* eye-line;
* body orientation;
* subject scale;
* lens family;
* camera height;
* background direction;
* light direction.

Intentional changes must respect editing grammar.

---

# 79. 180-degree rule

In scenes with:

* another character;
* a product interaction;
* a conversation;
* vehicle presentation;
* directional movement;

the camera should generally preserve the 180-degree axis.

Crossing the axis requires:

* a neutral transition;
* visible camera movement;
* a clear re-establishing shot;
* deliberate editorial intent.

---

# 80. 30-degree rule

Successive shots of Mei should normally differ by at least:

```text
Approximately 30 degrees
```

or by a meaningful framing change.

This reduces jump cuts and visual instability.

---

# 81. Continuity of scale

Within a matching sequence, Mei’s scale must remain coherent.

Prohibited:

* head size changing without camera movement;
* body scale changing between adjacent shots;
* product scale changing;
* inconsistent distance from background elements.

Scale continuity is a blocking requirement for professional output.

---

# 82. Background relationship

The camera must preserve:

* logical perspective;
* correct horizon;
* stable architecture;
* coherent depth;
* appropriate scale;
* realistic contact between Mei and the environment.

Backgrounds must not:

* bend around Mei;
* change between frames;
* move independently;
* create impossible vanishing points;
* make Mei appear pasted into the scene.

---

# 83. Horizon line

The horizon should remain:

* level by default;
* visually coherent;
* compatible with camera height;
* stable during movement.

A tilted horizon requires deliberate creative direction.

---

# 84. Ground contact

Full-body and walking shots must preserve:

* correct foot placement;
* stable ground plane;
* realistic shadow contact;
* no floating;
* no sinking;
* correct perspective.

Camera angle must not hide ground-contact defects when feet are narratively important.

---

# 85. Background separation

Mei should remain visually separated from the background through:

* lighting;
* depth of field;
* tonal contrast;
* color contrast;
* composition;
* controlled edge detail.

Separation must not create an artificial cut-out appearance.

---

# 86. Green-screen camera rules

For green-screen presenter footage:

* use stable framing;
* avoid excessive motion blur;
* preserve clean hair edges;
* avoid green wardrobe;
* avoid green reflections;
* maintain sufficient distance from the screen;
* minimize green spill;
* keep lighting even;
* preserve the full intended crop.

Recommended:

```text
Eye-level camera
Standard-to-short-telephoto perspective
Static or minimal movement
Moderate depth of field
```

---

# 87. Background replacement

When replacing a background, the camera model must match:

* camera height;
* lens perspective;
* horizon;
* depth;
* light direction;
* apparent subject scale;
* floor plane.

A mismatch creates an artificial composite and must be corrected.
