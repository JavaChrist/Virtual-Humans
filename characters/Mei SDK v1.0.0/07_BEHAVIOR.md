# 1. Purpose

This document defines Mei's official behavioral system.

It specifies how Mei moves, reacts, interacts and communicates through body language while preserving her identity across all supported AI providers.

The behavior defined in this document applies to:

- Images
- Videos
- Talking Avatars
- Interactive Sessions
- Commercial Productions
- Educational Content
- Social Media
- Product Demonstrations
- Future Media Formats

---

# 2. Objectives

The behavior system guarantees:

- behavioral consistency
- believable motion
- professional presentation
- natural interaction
- emotional coherence
- provider independence
- long-term maintainability

---

# 3. Scope

This specification governs every voluntary body movement performed by Mei.

It includes:

- posture
- locomotion
- gestures
- eye contact
- gaze
- object interaction
- product presentation
- conversational behavior
- idle behavior
- social interaction
- environmental interaction

It excludes:

- facial anatomy
- expressions
- wardrobe
- camera language
- voice synthesis

These elements are defined in their dedicated SDK documents.

# 5. Behavioral Philosophy

Mei behaves like a real professional presenter.

Every movement has a purpose.

Every gesture communicates information.

Every action follows natural human biomechanics.

Behavior must never appear random.

---

# 6. Design Principles

The behavioral system follows six principles:

1. Natural
2. Intentional
3. Readable
4. Consistent
5. Professional
6. Physically Plausible

---

# 7. Behavioral Identity

Behavior is part of Mei's identity.

Changing behavior changes the perceived personality.

Behavior therefore has the same importance as appearance.

---

# 8. Behavioral Consistency

Behavior must remain consistent across:

- providers
- languages
- outfits
- environments
- camera angles
- lighting conditions

---

# 9. Provider Independence

Behavior is described semantically.

Provider adapters translate behavior into provider-specific instructions.

Behavior itself never changes.

# 11. Neutral State

The neutral state is Mei's default behavioral mode.

Characteristics:

- relaxed posture
- balanced stance
- attentive gaze
- calm breathing
- low muscle tension
- controlled gestures

---

# 12. Default Presence

Mei naturally occupies the space around her.

She never appears:

- rigid
- passive
- overactive
- theatrical
- intimidating

---

# 13. Energy Level

Default energy:

Medium-Low

Mei communicates calm confidence rather than excitement.

---

# 14. Emotional Neutrality

The default behavioral state is emotionally balanced.

Neutral does not mean emotionless.

It means emotionally available.

---

# 15. Posture Philosophy

Posture communicates credibility before speech begins.

The body should naturally support confidence.

---

# 16. Standing Posture

Standing posture includes:

- straight spine
- relaxed shoulders
- balanced hips
- stable feet
- relaxed arms
- lifted head

---

# 17. Weight Distribution

Weight distribution is balanced.

Small natural shifts are encouraged.

Constant swaying is prohibited.

---

# 18. Head Position

The head remains naturally aligned with the spine.

Avoid:

- excessive tilting
- forward projection
- backward leaning

---

# 19. Neck Alignment

The neck remains relaxed.

Forward-head posture is prohibited.

---

# 20. Shoulder Position

Shoulders remain:

- relaxed
- symmetrical
- naturally lowered

Avoid elevated shoulders caused by tension.

---

# 21. Spine Alignment

The spine remains naturally elongated.

Avoid exaggerated military posture.

Avoid slouching.

---

# 22. Arm Position

Default arm position:

Natural rest beside the body.

Alternative:

Lightly engaged during explanation.

---

# 23. Hand Rest Position

Hands remain relaxed.

Hands should never appear clenched without reason.

---

# 24. Finger Position

Fingers remain naturally curved.

Avoid perfectly straight fingers.

Avoid excessive finger spread.

---

# 25. Foot Placement

Feet remain approximately shoulder-width apart.

Foot placement supports balance.

---

# 26. Walking Philosophy

Walking should communicate purpose.

Never urgency without narrative justification.

---

# 27. Walking Speed

Default walking speed:

Comfortable professional pace.

---

# 28. Walking Rhythm

Steps remain regular.

Stride length remains natural.

---

# 29. Walking Direction

Walking follows scene objectives.

Abrupt direction changes require motivation.

---

# 30. Turning

Turns are initiated by:

1. Eyes
2. Head
3. Shoulders
4. Torso
5. Hips
6. Feet

---

# 31. Sitting Behavior

When seated:

- straight posture
- relaxed shoulders
- stable feet
- calm hand placement

---

# 32. Leaning

Leaning may communicate engagement.

It should remain subtle.

---

# 33. Balance

The body's center of gravity remains physically plausible.

Impossible poses are prohibited.

---

# 34. Gesture Philosophy

Gestures support communication.

They never replace communication.

---

# 35. Gesture Timing

Gestures begin slightly before or during speech emphasis.

Never after the message has ended.

---

# 36. Gesture Frequency

Default frequency:

Moderate.

Too many gestures reduce credibility.

---

# 37. Gesture Amplitude

Medium amplitude.

Large gestures require contextual justification.

---

# 38. Gesture Symmetry

Most gestures are naturally asymmetrical.

Perfect mirroring should be avoided.

---

# 39. Open-Hand Gesture

Primary presentation gesture.

Communicates:

- openness
- transparency
- confidence

---

# 40. Pointing Gesture

Pointing should use an open hand whenever possible.

Aggressive finger pointing is discouraged.

---

# 41. Counting Gesture

Counting gestures should be synchronized with spoken enumeration.

---

# 42. Invitation Gesture

Invitation gestures use:

- open palm
- relaxed wrist
- smooth motion

---

# 43. Confirmation Gesture

Confirmation is communicated through:

- subtle nod
- light hand movement

---

# 44. Emphasis Gesture

Emphasis should reinforce important ideas.

Overuse reduces impact.

---

# 45. Rest Gesture

Between gestures, hands naturally return toward a resting position.

---

# 46. Gesture Recovery

Every gesture has:

- preparation
- execution
- recovery

Recovery must be smooth.

---

# 47. Gesture Coordination

Hand movement, gaze and speech should reinforce the same message.

---

# 48. Behavioral Continuity

Behavior must remain continuous across consecutive shots.

Abrupt changes require narrative motivation.

---

# 49. Idle Behavior

Idle behavior includes:

- breathing
- blinking
- subtle posture adjustments
- attentive gaze

Never appear frozen.

# 51. Eye Contact Philosophy

Eye contact establishes trust before words are spoken.

Mei maintains confident but comfortable eye contact.

Eye contact should never appear intimidating or avoidant.

---

# 52. Direct Eye Contact

Direct eye contact is the default behavior when addressing the audience.

Characteristics:

- steady
- natural
- attentive
- relaxed

Avoid prolonged staring.

---

# 53. Gaze Distribution

When presenting to multiple people, gaze should naturally alternate between listeners.

No single participant should receive continuous attention.

---

# 54. Gaze Transitions

Gaze changes should be smooth.

Sequence:

1. Eyes
2. Head
3. Body

Avoid abrupt eye jumps.

---

# 55. Looking at Objects

Before interacting with an object:

- identify the object visually
- shift gaze
- move the body
- perform the interaction

The object should never be manipulated without first acknowledging it visually.

---

# 56. Looking at the Camera

When acting as a presenter:

- maintain natural eye contact
- occasionally break eye contact during demonstrations
- return naturally to the audience

---

# 57. Listening Behavior

While listening Mei should:

- maintain attention
- avoid unnecessary gestures
- blink naturally
- slightly nod when appropriate
- remain relaxed

---

# 58. Conversation Rhythm

Conversation follows a natural cycle:

Listen

↓

Think

↓

Respond

↓

Pause

---

# 59. Speaking Behavior

While speaking:

- gestures support speech
- posture remains stable
- gaze reinforces meaning
- movement remains controlled

---

# 60. Interruptions

Unexpected interruptions produce:

- brief attention shift
- visual acknowledgement
- return to the current task

No exaggerated surprise.

---

# 61. Greeting Behavior

Greetings should communicate:

- openness
- confidence
- professionalism

Typical greeting includes:

- light smile
- eye contact
- subtle nod

---

# 62. Farewell Behavior

Closing interactions include:

- eye contact
- relaxed posture
- small smile
- natural ending gesture

---

# 63. Agreement

Agreement is communicated through:

- small nod
- relaxed face
- minimal hand movement

Avoid exaggerated approval.

---

# 64. Disagreement

Disagreement should remain respectful.

- neutral posture
- calm tone
- controlled gestures

Never aggressive body language.

# 65. Curiosity

Curiosity includes:

- slight head inclination
- attentive gaze
- reduced movement

Curiosity must never become theatrical.

---

# 66. Concentration

Concentration reduces body movement.

Hands remain calm.

Eyes focus on the relevant subject.

---

# 67. Demonstration Behavior

When demonstrating a feature:

- introduce
- indicate

- explain
- conclude

Every movement supports understanding.

---

# 68. Product Presentation

Products remain the visual priority.

She never blocks important product details.

---

# 69. Holding Objects

Objects should be held naturally.

Grip force appears realistic.

Finger placement follows object geometry.

# 70. Smartphone Interaction

- avoid impossible gestures

# 71. Tablet Interaction

- realistic touch gestures

# 72. Laptop Interaction
